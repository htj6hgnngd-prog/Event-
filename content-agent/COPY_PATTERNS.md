# VECTA Copy & Content Pattern Library — pass 2

This file converts the post audits into operational patterns. These are tools to choose between, not formulas to paste into every post. Every pattern must be grounded in the actual source materials.

## 1. Concept-led editorial caption
**Observed in:** Lou Escobar campaign presentation; Danil Golovkin’s “Ophelia” statement.
**Shape:** the visual premise → what two or more image elements mean together → credits if useful.
**Use when:** the series genuinely has a central concept, visual tension or metaphor.
**Avoid:** inventing symbolism after the fact; using grand language to make an ordinary set sound profound.
**VECTA example skeleton:** “В этой серии [specific object / styling / light] работает не как декор, а как [meaning demonstrated by the pictures].”
The agent must replace both placeholders with what the actual frames support.

## 2. Compact constraint-to-result
**Observed in:** Hannah Ladd Brown’s “No budget. My closet. This is what we made.”
**Shape:** concrete constraint → collaborators/decisive resource → result.
**Use when:** the production limitation is real and makes the outcome notable.
**Avoid:** generic claims like “nothing is impossible” or manufacturing hardship for engagement.

## 3. Show the editing decision
**Observed in:** Chris Ha’s full-scene/crop carousel.
**Shape:** original context → highlighted decision → final result → one practical takeaway or a specific question.
**Use when:** the difference is visible and teaches something about frame, camera position, light, crop, colour or retouching.
**Asset requirement:** include both actual states; do not substitute an illustration of the technique for the real source.
**Question quality:** ask what the viewer sees differently, not an automatic “which is your favorite?”

## 4. Technical BTS: problem → decision → consequence
**Observed in:** Joseph L. Hart’s lighting explanation; Alexey Andreev’s SFX-continuity caption.
**Shape:** exact production problem → one or two decisions → observable consequence → verified credits.
**Use when:** there is a real story of how a shot was made. Technical detail builds credibility when it resolves a problem.
**Avoid:** jargon with no context, gear lists that do not explain the result, unsupported settings, fake BTS details.

## 5. One resource, multiple outcomes
**Observed in:** Lindsay Adler / Adorama teaching post.
**Shape:** common constraint → multiple distinguishable techniques → visible examples → genuinely available follow-on tutorial.
**Use when:** VECTA has an actual breakdown, tutorial, case document or other resource worth delivering.
**CTA rule:** a keyword-comment CTA is a specific distribution system, not a default ending. Never promise a resource that does not exist or cannot be delivered.

## 6. Event story beyond the run sheet
**Observed in:** CandidShutters Media.
**Shape:** contrast between the obvious coverage shots and the overlooked human moments → explain why those moments matter to the client → actionable end.
**Possible real moment categories:** reaction, transition, exchange, pause, room settling, post-session conversation, sponsor interaction, detail with actual business relevance.
**Use when:** the brief contains real event material that can support this distinction.
**Avoid:** pretending any ordinary candid automatically tells a story. The moment must reveal something or fulfil a specific use.

## 7. Event-day BTS as a peer story
**Observed in:** Corpora Studios, with a concrete step-count statistic.
**Shape:** verifiable operational detail → recognizable friction or shared experience → question targeted at people who genuinely relate.
**Use when:** VECTA has accurate production facts such as crew count, zones covered, shot turnaround, timeline, number of interview setups or another real operational metric.
**Avoid:** fake numbers, irrelevant trivia, “comment below” prompts directed at an audience with no reason to answer. A public post with few comments is not proof the format is bad; sample size and audience relevance matter.

## 8. Content as an asset for the client's next action
**Observed in:** Lumetry Media, Tone Stockenstrom and Anthony B Media; the first emphasizes sponsor moments/audience engagement, the second a usable photo library, the third the downstream value of a crowded event.
**Shape:** specific client objective → proof from the event → where the asset can be used → next step if relevant.
**Use when:** the post’s goal is B2B trust or a qualified lead.
**Avoid:** long generic service lists. Name an actual deliverable (speaker portraits, sponsor interactions, interview snippets, recap, vertical edits, same-day selects) only when it is part of the service being offered.

## 9. Production team as protagonists
**Observed in:** Kainaat Uppal’s shoot recap and LANE Casting UK’s editorial credits.
**Shape:** one or two true details from the process → why they mattered → clear credits grouped by role.
**Use when:** the production involved meaningful collaboration or a detail made the shoot distinctive.
**Avoid:** list every contributor without order, omit important collaborators, tag handles without checking spelling/permission, or call a detail “behind the scenes” without seeing it.

## 10. Education that is visual rather than merely explanatory
**Observed in:** Adorama / Lindsay Adler and Chris Ha.
**Shape:** one tangible visual problem → two or three observable solutions → show each one → concise lesson.
**Use when:** visual difference is understandable at a glance and the audience can learn from it.
**Avoid:** educational captions that explain lighting in abstract terms while the carousel shows unrelated images.

## 11. Tone and quality tiers
Classify the proposed caption before writing:
- **Editorial concept:** one premise; fewer words, high image-specificity.
- **Personal/process story:** a genuine event or constraint; narrative length earned by details.
- **Technical/educational:** problem and actual method; precision over mood.
- **B2B/value:** business outcome and proof; direct language, no generic luxury adjectives.
- **Community conversation:** one clear, answerable question rooted in the image or shared work.
- **Credits-first:** minimal copy is enough when the work is self-explanatory and credits are the main missing information.

A caption can combine two tiers, but should have one primary job.

## 12. Anti-pattern library
Reject or rewrite these:
- **Universal poetic claim:** can be attached to any shoot (“the moments that stay forever”).
- **False profundity:** symbolic meaning not supported by creator/client or the actual visual series.
- **Engagement bait:** generic “which do you like?” repeated regardless of the material.
- **Feature dump:** gear/model/service lists with no explanation of what mattered.
- **B2B fog:** “energy, magic, story, premium” without proof, deliverable or use case.
- **Unverifiable production narrative:** inventing how hard the shoot was or what happened off camera.
- **Overlong sales close:** piling multiple promises and locations after an opening that was already clear.
- **Algorithmic pseudo-metric:** repeating someone else’s public likes or a self-reported ratio as evidence of what will work for VECTA.

## 13. Evaluation loop
For each VECTA post, capture after publication if account analytics become available:
- format and actual cover;
- copy tier and opening mechanism;
- whether it used a known pattern;
- reach/impressions/watch time/completion/saves/shares/comments/profile visits/enquiries, whichever are actually available;
- comparison against VECTA’s own baseline and the closest prior posts;
- confounding factors and sample size.

Do not conclude a pattern is “best” after one post. A pattern earns higher confidence only after repeated tests in comparable conditions.
